Where India chooses its future

A map of what millions of Class 10 decisions add up to, where the country's biggest exams concentrate, and why guiding each choice well is a matter of nation building.

By the DISHA Career Lab team23 September 20267 min read

Every year, around the time Class 10 results come out, millions of Indian families make one decision that shapes the next decade: Science, Commerce or Arts. It is often made in a few anxious weeks, based on marks, neighbours' advice and whichever coaching centre has the loudest billboard.

We mapped what those decisions add up to across the country. The picture is striking, and it tells us something important about how India builds its future talent.

Two streams, one bottleneck

Nationally, Science and Arts each draw roughly four in ten students after Class 10. Commerce has stayed at about 14% for a decade, according to the Ministry of Education's study of board results. That flatness is surprising in an economy increasingly driven by finance, entrepreneurship, GST-era compliance and digital business.

Behind the streams sit the entrance exams, and their scale in the 2026 cycle is enormous.

  • 22.8 lakhcandidates registered for NEET UG
  • 16.0 lakhunique candidates registered for JEE Main across both sessions
  • 8.2 lakhapplied for UPSC Civil Services; 5.49 lakh sat the Prelims for 933 vacancies

That is roughly one civil services post for every 880 applicants.

Geography is destiny, for now

The most revealing part of the map is how differently states choose. In Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Tamil Nadu, more than 60% of students take Science, and only about 2% choose Arts. In Gujarat and Tripura, over 82% choose Arts, and in Punjab and Rajasthan it is above 70%. Science uptake in Punjab, Haryana and Assam is only about 17%. Karnataka and Goa are the rare balanced states, with students spread almost evenly across the major streams.

The exams have their own geography too. Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan and Bihar together account for 41% of NEET registrations. Twelve of the 26 students who scored a perfect 100 percentile in JEE Main 2026 came from Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, and four from Rajasthan. For UPSC, Delhi was the largest Prelims centre with 70,885 candidates, followed by Hyderabad with 44,209 and Patna with 39,147.

Six hubs stand out: Kota, Delhi NCR, the Lucknow–Patna corridor, Mumbai–Pune, Hyderabad and Bengaluru. Together they have become the country's talent sorting machines.

What the map doesn't show

A map of choices is not a map of fit. When a state sends over 60% of its students into Science, or over 70% into Arts, that is rarely because interests happen to cluster so neatly. More often it reflects local tradition, school capacity, coaching availability and family expectation.

The cost of that mismatch is real. Lakhs of students prepare for years for exams where only a small fraction can succeed. Some of them would have thrived in design, law, economics, public policy or entrepreneurship. Meanwhile Commerce, a stream directly linked to business formation and the formal economy, has not grown in ten years.

A nation of 1.4 billion needs doctors and engineers. It equally needs economists, designers, teachers, researchers, founders, administrators and skilled professionals. When stream choice follows the crowd rather than the child, we lose that breadth.

Where DISHA comes in

DISHA Career Lab was built on a simple belief: the right stream is the one that fits the student, not the one that fits the neighbourhood.

Our 5-D Assessment looks beyond marks to help each student understand their interests, strengths and working style, and which core streams suit them. It then gives them a clear roadmap to their goals. It turns an anxious guess into an informed decision that students, parents and schools can discuss together.

Imagine the map redrawn with better guidance:

  • Fewer default Science choices in states where it is the norm. Students with genuine interest still pursue it, and others find paths that fit them better.
  • More confident Arts and Commerce choices, made with a plan rather than by elimination.
  • Less wasted preparation in the exam hubs, because students who enter the JEE, NEET or UPSC race do so with conviction.
  • A more balanced talent pipeline feeding every sector India needs to grow.

That is how better individual decisions become nation building. Each student placed in the right stream is more likely to finish, excel and contribute. Multiplied across millions, that shapes the country's workforce for decades.

Start with self-understanding

If you are a student approaching Class 10 results, or a parent or teacher guiding one, understand the student before choosing the stream. The map of India's choices is redrawn every year, one student at a time.
